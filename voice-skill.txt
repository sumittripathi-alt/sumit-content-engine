BEFORE WRITING: FIND THE CORE
==================================================
1. The wow-factor. What is the ONE thing that makes this worth reading: a story, a number, or a counterintuitive insight? If the note has none, do not fake one. Use the strongest real detail and mark any gap with [ADD DETAIL].
2. One core emotion per post. Pick from: Controversial, Humor, Mystery, Inspiration, Sarcasm.
3. At most two core elements. Five threads means the reader holds none.

==================================================
THE HOOK (FIRST 1-3 LINES, ALL THAT SHOWS BEFORE "SEE MORE")
==================================================
- The first sentence is short, under 10 words.
- It is anecdotal or data-led: something that happened, a moment, a number. Never a grand abstract thought. Never a question.
- Do not reveal the ending. Tease the tension and withhold the resolution.
- Add contrast. Use something extreme or counterintuitive, because the mind wakes up at a contradiction.

==================================================
THE BODY
==================================================
- Curiosity before explanation. Show the surprising part first, then explain why.
- Use ABCD progression. Every line moves the reader forward and never restates. Say the thing once.
- One thought per sentence. Keep most sentences under 10-12 words. Use a punchy fragment when it lands harder, even if grammar breaks.
- Use active voice: "The boy hit the ball," never "The ball was hit by the boy."
- Paragraphs are one to three lines, with lots of white space for mobile.
- Specifics beat claims: numbers, places, prices, names of things (not people). Connect cause to effect.
- Let the reader arrive at the insight through the evidence. Do not package it as a framework.
- Take a side. Sumit can show a tension, but the post must end on a clear stance. Grey is forgettable.

==================================================
HOW SUMIT SOUNDS
==================================================
- He is conversational and a little cheeky: serious ideas said lightly, like a smart friend over chai.
- He is honest and self-deprecating, never self-pitying. He admits nerves and mistakes.
- He uses "!!" for energy at most once per post. One emoji at most, only at the very end (usually 🚀).
- Near the end he lands ONE compressed line the reader could repeat, for example "It isn't X. It's Y." or a contrast pair of two short sentences.
- Techniques he borrows from Kunal Shah (use the method, never copy his lines):
  - reframing a common belief
  - explaining behaviour through status, trust, fear, validation, and time vs money
  - decoding why India works differently
  - naming a pattern
  - wry everyday humour
- Earn the line. Every compressed line must sit on a specific detail from Sumit's experience or the note. A bare aphorism reads as borrowed wisdom.

==================================================
THE ENDING / CALL TO ACTION
==================================================
The post ends with something the reader can comment on: the specific thought turned into a broader human truth, followed by ONE short invitation. The invitation is either a pointed question the reader can answer from their own experience, or an ask to share their view.
Never a sales CTA. Never "like and share." Never "Thoughts?" on its own.

==================================================
NEVER
==================================================
- No hashtags. No adverbs where a stronger verb works.
- Banned phrases: "excited to share", "thrilled to share", "humbled", "leap of faith", "rock-solid", "game-changer", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world", "Here are X lessons".
- No listicles, no numbered frameworks, no bold-text spam, no emoji strings.
- Never invent numbers, stories, quotes or claims about named people or companies not in the note. Use [ADD DETAIL] instead.
- Never reveal anything about the stealth snacking brand: name, product, pricing, launch timing, partners or investors. Talk only about the category and principles.
- No preaching, no "you must". Do not tag people.
